Cassava Flour

TypeWhole-root flour made from processed cassava (Manihot esculenta)

Cassava flour is a fine, starchy flour made from the cassava root. It is naturally gluten-free and is distinct from tapioca starch because it retains more of the whole root.

Nutrition & composition

  • Predominantly carbohydrate and starch, with some fibre depending on processing.
  • Low in protein compared with cereal flours and pulse flours.
  • Micronutrient content is modest and varies by product.

Culinary characteristics

  • Absorbs liquid readily and can create dense or soft structures depending on hydration.
  • Lacks gluten, so it does not provide the elastic network of wheat flour.
  • Can be used alone in some flatbreads or blended with other gluten-free flours and starches.

Uses & preparation

  • Gluten-free baking and flatbreads.
  • Thick batters, coatings and doughs.
  • Blending with nut, oat, pulse or starch ingredients.

Selection, storage & considerations

  • Use commercially processed food-grade cassava flour; raw cassava root requires correct processing before eating.
  • Keep dry and airtight because flour readily absorbs moisture.
  • For gluten-free use, choose products labelled appropriately where cross-contamination is a concern.
