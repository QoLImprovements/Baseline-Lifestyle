Rosemary

TypeWoody culinary herb (Salvia rosmarinus)

Rosemary is a strongly aromatic woody herb with pine-like, resinous and slightly bitter notes. It tolerates prolonged heat better than delicate leafy herbs.

Nutrition & composition

  • Contains aromatic plant compounds and micronutrients by weight, but ordinary culinary amounts are small.
  • Its main practical contribution is flavour rather than macronutrients.
  • Fresh and dried rosemary differ in intensity and texture.

Culinary characteristics

  • Strong flavour stands up to roasting and long cooking.
  • Pairs especially well with potatoes, mushrooms, pulses and roasted vegetables.
  • Whole woody leaves can remain firm, so finely chopping or using an infusion may improve texture.

Uses & preparation

  • Roasts and tray bakes.
  • Soups, stews, breads and grain dishes.
  • Herb blends, marinades and infused cooking liquids.

Selection, storage & considerations

  • Dried rosemary should be stored airtight away from light.
  • Use more cautiously than mild herbs because it can dominate a dish.
  • Concentrated rosemary essential oil is not interchangeable with culinary rosemary.
