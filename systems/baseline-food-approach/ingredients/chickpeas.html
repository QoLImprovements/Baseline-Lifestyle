Chickpeas

TypePulse / legume (Cicer arietinum)

Chickpeas are firm, nutty pulses eaten whole or milled into flour. They combine starch with plant protein and fibre, making them useful as both a carbohydrate and protein component.

Nutrition & composition

  • Provides plant protein, dietary fibre and slowly digested starch.
  • Contributes folate, iron, magnesium, potassium and other minerals.
  • Low in saturated fat and naturally cholesterol-free.

Culinary characteristics

  • Cooked chickpeas range from firm to creamy depending on cooking time.
  • Their cooking liquid and mashed flesh can add body to soups, dips and sauces.
  • Roasting creates a drier, nuttier texture.

Uses & preparation

  • Curries, stews, soups, salads and grain bowls.
  • Hummus, spreads and mashed fillings.
  • Roasted snacks and chickpea-flour preparations.

Selection, storage & considerations

  • Dried chickpeas need soaking and thorough cooking unless using another validated cooking method.
  • Canned chickpeas are ready to use after draining/rinsing if desired.
  • Pulses can increase digestive gas for some people; gradual intake and thorough cooking can improve tolerance.
