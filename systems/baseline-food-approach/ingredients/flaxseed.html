Flaxseed

TypeOil-rich seed (Linum usitatissimum), also called linseed

Flaxseed is a small oil-rich seed valued for its fibre, alpha-linolenic acid (ALA) omega-3 fat and lignans. It can be used whole, but grinding changes both texture and nutrient accessibility.

Nutrition & composition

  • Provides fibre, plant protein and predominantly unsaturated fat.
  • One of the richest commonly eaten plant sources of ALA omega-3 fatty acid.
  • Contains lignans and minerals such as magnesium and manganese.

Culinary characteristics

  • Ground flax absorbs water and forms a gel-like mixture.
  • Adds a mild nutty flavour and fine texture to oats, baking and batters.
  • Whole seeds add texture but may pass through digestion less completely than ground seed.

Uses & preparation

  • Porridge, cereals, smoothies and yoghurt alternatives.
  • Baking, crackers and breads.
  • Ground flax mixed with water as a binder in some egg-free recipes.

Selection, storage & considerations

  • Ground flax oxidises faster than whole seed; keep it sealed, cool and preferably refrigerated after grinding.
  • Increase high-fibre foods gradually and take adequate fluid if your usual fibre intake is low.
  • Check product labelling where gluten cross-contamination matters.
