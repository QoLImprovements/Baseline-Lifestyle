Peas

TypeGreen legume / vegetable (Pisum sativum)

Green peas sit between a vegetable and a pulse nutritionally: they are sweet and tender but contain more starch and protein than many non-starchy vegetables.

Nutrition & composition

  • Provides fibre, plant protein and carbohydrate.
  • Contributes vitamin C, vitamin K, folate and several minerals.
  • Frozen peas retain good nutritional value and are convenient because they are usually frozen soon after processing.

Culinary characteristics

  • Naturally sweet with a tender skin and soft interior.
  • Brief cooking preserves colour and a firmer texture; longer cooking makes them softer and easier to mash.
  • Blend smoothly into soups, sauces and purées.

Uses & preparation

  • Soups, stews, curries and hotpots.
  • Pasta alternatives, grain dishes and salads.
  • Mash, purées, fritters and mixed vegetables.

Selection, storage & considerations

  • Frozen peas can usually be cooked directly from frozen.
  • Avoid prolonged overcooking if bright colour and fresh flavour are desired.
  • Store frozen peas continuously frozen and reseal the bag to reduce freezer burn.
