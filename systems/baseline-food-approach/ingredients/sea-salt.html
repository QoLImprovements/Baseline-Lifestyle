Sea Salt

TypeFood-grade sodium chloride produced from seawater

Sea salt is primarily sodium chloride. Crystal size, moisture and trace-mineral content can change flavour perception and measuring behaviour, but sea salt should not be treated as a nutritionally special alternative to other salt.

Nutrition & composition

  • Supplies sodium and chloride, both essential electrolytes, but typical diets can easily provide more sodium than needed.
  • Trace minerals are generally present in amounts too small to make sea salt a meaningful mineral supplement.
  • Iodine is present only when the product is iodised or naturally contains a declared amount; sea salt is not automatically iodised.

Culinary characteristics

  • Salt enhances flavour, suppresses some bitterness and affects texture in many foods.
  • Fine and coarse crystals occupy different volumes, so teaspoon measures are not interchangeable by weight.
  • Adding salt gradually gives better control than relying on a fixed volume measure across brands.

Uses & preparation

  • General seasoning during cooking.
  • Finishing foods where crystal texture is desired.
  • Brines, baking and doughs where salt also affects structure.

Selection, storage & considerations

  • Keep dry and sealed to prevent clumping.
  • Use weight rather than volume when precision matters.
  • Do not assume speciality or coloured salts provide meaningful nutritional advantages over ordinary food-grade salt.
