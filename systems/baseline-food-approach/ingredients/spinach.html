Spinach

TypeLeafy green vegetable (Spinacia oleracea)

Spinach is a tender leafy green that can be eaten raw or cooked. Heat dramatically reduces its volume as water is released and the leaves collapse.

Nutrition & composition

  • Provides folate, vitamin K, carotenoids and vitamin C, with amounts affected by cooking and storage.
  • Contains non-haem iron, magnesium and other minerals.
  • Also contains oxalate, which can bind some minerals within the food.

Culinary characteristics

  • Raw leaves are tender and mildly earthy; cooked spinach becomes soft and more concentrated in flavour.
  • Frozen spinach is compact and convenient for soups, stews, curries and sauces.
  • Excess water can be squeezed from cooked or thawed spinach when a drier texture is needed.

Uses & preparation

  • Salads and sandwiches when raw.
  • Soups, curries, hotpots, sauces and stews.
  • Fillings, grain dishes, pulses and savoury bakes.

Selection, storage & considerations

  • Wash fresh spinach thoroughly unless sold ready-to-eat.
  • Frozen spinach should remain frozen until use and be heated according to product guidance.
  • Because spinach is high in vitamin K, people taking warfarin are generally advised to keep intake reasonably consistent rather than making abrupt major changes.
