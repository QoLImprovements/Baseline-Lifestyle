Almonds

TypeTree nut / edible seed of Prunus dulcis

Almonds are firm, mildly sweet tree nuts used whole, chopped, ground, toasted or blended. They combine a high-energy fat profile with protein, fibre and a useful range of micronutrients.

Nutrition & composition

  • Rich in unsaturated fat and provides plant protein and fibre.
  • A notable food source of vitamin E and magnesium, with smaller amounts of several B vitamins and minerals.
  • Whole almonds retain their fibre-rich structure; nut butter and flour change texture but not the underlying ingredient.

Culinary characteristics

  • Crunchy when raw or toasted; grinding releases oils and creates a paste.
  • Toasting deepens flavour and aroma.
  • Ground almonds add tenderness and density to baking and can also thicken or enrich sauces.

Uses & preparation

  • Snacking, cereals, porridge and salads.
  • Nut butters, ground-almond baking and desserts.
  • Sauces, coatings, granola and savoury toppings.

Selection, storage & considerations

  • Keep dry and sealed; refrigeration or freezing can extend freshness because the oils can become rancid.
  • Skin-on almonds provide additional fibre and plant compounds.
  • Tree-nut allergen; cross-contamination matters where allergy control is required.
