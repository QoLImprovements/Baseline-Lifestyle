Creamy Maris Piper & Peanut Potato Bake

Batch: 1×
Batch Multiplier
Measurement Type
Use:
Batch quantity: 1 • Measurement: Standard Variance

Purpose

An oil-free baked potato dish with tender Maris Piper slices coated in a creamy peanut and herb sauce, made using the BAKE function on a Ninja Foodi 8-in-1 PossibleCooker.

Recipe Profile

Bake function • vegan • gluten-free when ingredients are verified • no added oil, onion, garlic, milk or soya milk • base batch serves approximately 4 • BAKE at 180°C for approximately 60–75 minutes, plus resting time. Equipment: Ninja Foodi PossibleCooker or equivalent with a supported BAKE mode. Allergen: peanuts. Timing is an estimate for this adapted recipe, not a verified appliance-tested result.

How the Recipe Works

Thinly sliced Maris Piper potatoes soften and release starch as they cook. Natural peanut butter gives the sauce its body and fat without adding extracted oil. Tapioca starch binds the liquid, while herbes de Provence and lemon balance the nutty flavour.

Design Rationale

The sauce uses only water and peanut butter rather than dairy or plant milk, and the ingredient quantities are designed for a relatively thick bake instead of a watery casserole. Covered cooking prioritises tenderness in a multicooker heated from its base and sides.

Variables & Fixed Elements

Keep the base ratio at 800 g potato, 50 g peanut butter, 250 ml water and 10 g tapioca starch. Slice thickness, potato packing and appliance heating can alter cooking time and consistency. Use the total 250 ml water allocation, reserving 20 ml cold to disperse the tapioca.

Limitations & Trade-offs

Unlike a fan oven, the PossibleCooker does not brown the surface like a classic gratin. Tapioca may turn gummy if substantially increased. Maris Piper slices can break apart if stirred; use a knife test rather than stirring. Check actual tenderness rather than relying on the estimated timer.

Ingredients (View)

  • Potatoes & peanut sauce
  • 800 g Maris Piper potatoes, sliced 2–3 mm thick
  • 50 g natural (100% peanuts) peanut butter
  • 250 ml total water
  • 10 g tapioca starch
  • 1½ tsp herbes de Provence
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • ½ tsp, or to taste sea salt
  • Ground black pepper, to taste (optional)

Method

  1. Slice: Wash the Maris Piper potatoes and slice evenly to 2–3 mm. Keep the skins if desired. Do not rinse the cut slices.
  2. Whisk the sauce: Reserve 20 ml of the measured water cold. Gradually whisk the remaining 230 ml warm water into the natural peanut butter until smooth.
  3. Disperse the tapioca: Stir the tapioca starch into the reserved 20 ml cold water to form a lump-free slurry. Whisk the slurry into the peanut sauce before heating.
  4. Season: Mix in the herbes de Provence, lemon juice, salt and optional black pepper.
  5. Layer: Arrange the potato slices in overlapping layers in the Ninja cooking pot. Pour the sauce evenly over the potatoes, easing it between layers. Do not add oil.
  6. Bake covered: Select BAKE at 180°C with the oven-safe glass lid fitted. Start with 60 minutes; the expected total is approximately 60–75 minutes.
  7. Check tenderness: From about 50 minutes, insert a knife into the centre. The potato layers are ready when the knife passes through easily. Continue baking if there is resistance. If the sauce dries around the edges, add a small splash of hot water.
  8. Finish if needed: If the potatoes are tender but the sauce is still thin, bake uncovered for another 5–10 minutes while watching closely to prevent scorching at the base.
  9. Rest and serve: Turn the heat off and rest for 10 minutes so the sauce can settle before portioning. Taste and adjust seasoning.

Serving & Use

  • Base batch: approximately 4 servings as a side, or fewer as a main dish.
  • Serve warm; allow the sauce to settle before cutting or lifting out the potato layers.
  • Peanut flavour is noticeable; this is not a neutral, dairy-style gratin.

Body & Sensory Notes

  • Texture: soft layered potato with a creamy, slightly glossy sauce; Maris Piper may break down at the edges.
  • Target consistency: sauce coats the potatoes rather than pooling as thin liquid; the final set improves during resting.
  • Readiness: use the centre knife test as the main indicator; times vary with slice thickness and appliance load.
  • Equipment note: the Ninja PossibleCooker heats from below and the sides, so the top will remain relatively pale.
