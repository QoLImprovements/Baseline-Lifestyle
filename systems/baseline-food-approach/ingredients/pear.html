Pear

TypeFruit (Pyrus species)

Pears are water-rich fruits with a softening, aromatic flesh and a sweetness that develops as they ripen. Varieties differ in graininess, juiciness and how well they hold shape when cooked.

Nutrition & composition

  • Provides carbohydrate, water and dietary fibre, especially with the skin.
  • Contains vitamin C and a range of plant compounds in modest amounts.
  • Whole pear provides more fibre than juice or strained purée.

Culinary characteristics

  • Ripe pears are soft, fragrant and juicy; firmer fruit can be poached or baked while retaining shape.
  • Pairs well with oats, nuts, warm spices and acidic ingredients.
  • Can be grated or puréed to add moisture and natural sweetness.

Uses & preparation

  • Fresh fruit, porridge and breakfast bowls.
  • Poached, baked or stewed fruit.
  • Salads, desserts, baking and purées.

Selection, storage & considerations

  • Many pears ripen best at room temperature and can then be refrigerated.
  • Handle ripe pears gently because they bruise easily.
  • Cut surfaces brown; acid can slow oxidation.
