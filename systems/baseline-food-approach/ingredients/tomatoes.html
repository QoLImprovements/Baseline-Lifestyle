Tomatoes

TypeFruit used as a vegetable (Solanum lycopersicum)

Tomatoes combine acidity, sweetness, savoury glutamate and high water content. Variety and ripeness determine whether they are best for raw eating, roasting, sauces or long cooking.

Nutrition & composition

  • Provides vitamin C, potassium, folate and carotenoid pigments.
  • Red tomatoes are a major dietary source of lycopene.
  • Whole tomatoes provide fibre; concentrated tomato products contain less water and therefore more tomato solids per spoonful.

Culinary characteristics

  • Fresh tomatoes add water and bright acidity; long cooking concentrates sweetness and savouriness.
  • Roasting drives off water and develops deeper flavours.
  • Acidic tomato preparations can balance rich or starchy foods.

Uses & preparation

  • Salads, sandwiches and salsas.
  • Soups, stews, curries and hotpots.
  • Sauces, roasted dishes and baked preparations.

Selection, storage & considerations

  • Store according to ripeness; refrigeration slows deterioration but can dull flavour in some ripe tomatoes.
  • Discard mouldy or leaking fruit.
  • Fresh, frozen, canned and concentrated forms are not texturally interchangeable, so choose the form for the intended result.
