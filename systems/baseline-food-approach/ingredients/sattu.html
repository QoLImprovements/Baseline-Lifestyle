Sattu

TypeRoasted pulse flour, commonly roasted chickpea / Bengal gram flour

Sattu is a traditional roasted flour most commonly made from chickpeas or Bengal gram, though commercial blends can contain other grains or pulses. Roasting gives it a nutty flavour and makes it convenient for quick preparations.

Nutrition & composition

  • Pure chickpea sattu provides plant protein, fibre and carbohydrate.
  • Also contributes minerals and B vitamins associated with pulses.
  • Nutrition varies substantially if the product is blended with cereals or other pulses.

Culinary characteristics

  • Roasted flavour is deeper and drier than raw pulse flour.
  • Absorbs liquid quickly and can form thick drinks, doughs or fillings.
  • Can be used without the same long cooking needed for whole dried chickpeas, but preparation should still follow the product instructions.

Uses & preparation

  • Savoury drinks and porridges.
  • Flatbread fillings and dough mixtures.
  • Thickening or enriching savoury batters and snacks.

Selection, storage & considerations

  • Check the ingredient list because 'sattu' does not always mean 100% chickpea.
  • For gluten-free use, choose a product that is explicitly free from gluten-containing cereal contamination.
  • Store dry and airtight to prevent moisture pickup and stale flavours.
