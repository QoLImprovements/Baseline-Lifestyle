Carrot

TypeRoot vegetable (Daucus carota)

Carrots are sweet, crisp roots that become tender and more intensely sweet when cooked. They are useful raw, roasted, simmered, blended or grated.

Nutrition & composition

  • A major dietary source of beta-carotene, which the body can convert to vitamin A.
  • Provides fibre, potassium and smaller amounts of several vitamins and minerals.
  • Orange, purple, yellow and other varieties differ in their dominant plant pigments.

Culinary characteristics

  • Raw carrots are crisp and mildly sweet; cooking softens the fibre and brings out sweetness.
  • Roasting encourages browning; simmering produces a clean, soft vegetable base.
  • Blends smoothly into soups, sauces and purées.

Uses & preparation

  • Raw snacks, salads and slaws.
  • Roasts, soups, stews, curries and tray bakes.
  • Purées, sauces, baking and mixed vegetable dishes.

Selection, storage & considerations

  • Choose firm roots; refrigeration helps retain crispness.
  • Scrubbing is often enough for clean young carrots; peeling is optional.
  • Cut size strongly affects cooking time and final texture.
