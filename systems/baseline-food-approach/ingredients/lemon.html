Lemon

TypeCitrus fruit (Citrus limon)

Lemon provides sharp acidity, fresh citrus aroma and a small amount of natural sweetness. Juice, zest and whole segments behave differently and can be used separately.

Nutrition & composition

  • Lemon flesh and juice provide vitamin C and small amounts of other micronutrients.
  • Whole fruit and pulp contain more fibre than strained juice.
  • Citric acid is responsible for much of the characteristic acidity.

Culinary characteristics

  • Acidity brightens flavour and can reduce the amount of salt or sweetness a dish seems to need.
  • Juice can balance rich, nutty, starchy or earthy foods.
  • Zest supplies aromatic citrus oils without the same acidity as the juice.

Uses & preparation

  • Dressings, sauces, soups and pulses.
  • Finishing vegetables, grains and savoury dishes.
  • Baking, desserts, drinks and fruit preparations.

Selection, storage & considerations

  • Choose firm fruit that feels heavy for its size for good juice yield.
  • Wash before zesting, especially if the peel will be eaten.
  • Frequent prolonged contact with acidic drinks or undiluted juice can contribute to dental enamel erosion.
