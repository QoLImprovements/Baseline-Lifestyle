Peanut Butter

TypeLegume-based spread made from ground peanuts (Arachis hypogaea)

Peanut butter is a concentrated paste of roasted or raw peanuts. It is a substantial food ingredient in its own right: energy-dense, rich in unsaturated fat and protein, and useful across both savoury and sweet cooking.

Nutrition & composition

  • Provides plant protein, mostly unsaturated fat and dietary fibre.
  • Contributes nutrients including niacin, vitamin E, magnesium, phosphorus and other minerals.
  • Products with added sugar, palm fat or salt can differ considerably from 100% peanut butter.

Culinary characteristics

  • Creamy or crunchy textures are available; both carry a pronounced roasted-nut flavour.
  • Combines with water, stock or acidic ingredients to make emulsified sauces and dressings.
  • Adds richness and body, but its culinary value extends well beyond thickening.

Uses & preparation

  • Spreads, porridge, oats and fruit pairings.
  • Peanut sauces, dressings, soups, curries and stews.
  • Baking, desserts, smoothies and energy-dense snacks.

Selection, storage & considerations

  • Natural peanut butter often separates; stir the oil back through before use.
  • Store according to the label and protect from heat and prolonged air exposure.
  • Peanut is one of the UK's regulated major allergens.
