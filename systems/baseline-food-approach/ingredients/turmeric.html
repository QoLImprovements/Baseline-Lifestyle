Turmeric

TypeGround rhizome spice (Curcuma longa)

Turmeric is an earthy, warm and slightly bitter spice best known for its intense yellow-orange pigment. It is used primarily for flavour and colour in ordinary cooking.

Nutrition & composition

  • Contains curcuminoid pigments and other plant compounds.
  • Culinary serving sizes are small, so turmeric is not normally a major source of macronutrients.
  • Evidence about concentrated turmeric or curcumin supplements should not be automatically applied to normal food amounts.

Culinary characteristics

  • Strong pigment colours foods even at modest quantities.
  • Earthy bitterness works best balanced with other spices, acidity, sweetness or fat.
  • Blooms well during cooking in soups, curries and sauces.

Uses & preparation

  • Curries, dals, soups and stews.
  • Rice, grain dishes and roasted vegetables.
  • Spice blends, savoury drinks and sauces.

Selection, storage & considerations

  • Store airtight, dry and away from light.
  • Turmeric readily stains porous surfaces, fabrics and some plastics.
  • Use culinary powder as food; concentrated supplements are a different product with different dosing considerations.
