White Mushrooms

TypeCultivated fungus (Agaricus bisporus)

White button mushrooms are the young form of Agaricus bisporus, the same species that becomes chestnut/cremini and portobello mushrooms at later stages. They provide a mild savoury flavour that intensifies with browning.

Nutrition & composition

  • Provides fibre, B vitamins and minerals such as selenium and copper.
  • Contains some plant-quality protein relative to its low energy content, though mushrooms are not a high-protein food by dry-bean standards.
  • Vitamin D content is usually low unless mushrooms have been exposed to ultraviolet light.

Culinary characteristics

  • High water content means mushrooms initially release moisture when heated.
  • Browning after moisture evaporates creates deeper savoury flavour.
  • Blend smoothly into sauces and soups but can also retain a meaty bite when sliced and browned.

Uses & preparation

  • Soups, sauces, stews and risotto-style dishes.
  • Roasts, stir-fries, fillings and toppings.
  • Blended mushroom bases and savoury spreads.

Selection, storage & considerations

  • Store refrigerated with some airflow rather than sealed wet.
  • Brush or rinse briefly and dry; prolonged soaking can add unnecessary surface water.
  • Discard mushrooms that are slimy, strongly spoiled-smelling or mouldy.
