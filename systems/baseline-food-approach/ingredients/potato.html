Potato

TypeStarchy tuber (Solanum tuberosum)

Potatoes are versatile starchy tubers used as a principal carbohydrate food across many cuisines. Variety and cooking method determine whether they become fluffy, waxy, crisp, creamy or firm.

Nutrition & composition

  • Provides carbohydrate mainly as starch, plus potassium and vitamin C.
  • Skin-on potatoes provide more fibre than peeled potatoes.
  • Cooling cooked potato can increase resistant starch, although the amount depends on preparation and reheating.

Culinary characteristics

  • Floury varieties break down readily for mash and fluffy textures; waxy varieties hold their shape better.
  • Boiling, steaming, baking, roasting and pressure cooking all produce distinct textures.
  • Starch released from cooked potato can bind and thicken, but that is only one of many culinary roles.

Uses & preparation

  • Boiled, baked, roasted and mashed dishes.
  • Soups, stews, curries, salads and tray bakes.
  • Dumplings, patties, fillings and blended preparations.

Selection, storage & considerations

  • Store cool, dark and ventilated rather than in strong light.
  • Green areas and substantial sprouting can indicate elevated glycoalkaloids; avoid eating heavily green or bitter potatoes.
  • Choose variety according to the texture required rather than treating all potatoes as interchangeable.
