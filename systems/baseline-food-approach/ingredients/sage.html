Sage

TypeAromatic culinary herb (Salvia officinalis)

Sage has an assertive savoury, earthy and slightly camphor-like flavour. It is particularly effective with rich, starchy or creamy foods where a small amount can provide strong aromatic contrast.

Nutrition & composition

  • Contains aromatic plant compounds and micronutrients, although serving sizes are normally small.
  • Nutritional contribution is secondary to its seasoning role.
  • Dried sage is more concentrated by weight than fresh leaves.

Culinary characteristics

  • Powerful flavour can become bitter or medicinal if overused.
  • Fat and heat carry its aroma effectively.
  • Works well with beans, squash, potatoes and savoury sauces.

Uses & preparation

  • Stuffings, bean dishes and vegetable roasts.
  • Creamy or buttery-style sauces and pasta alternatives.
  • Herb blends, soups and savoury baking.

Selection, storage & considerations

  • Store dried sage airtight and away from heat and light.
  • Add modestly, taste, then increase if needed.
  • Use culinary leaf rather than concentrated essential oil in food preparations.
