Apple

TypeFruit (Malus domestica)

Apples are crisp, water-rich fruits ranging from tart to sweet. Their flavour, firmness and cooking behaviour vary substantially by cultivar, which makes them useful both raw and cooked.

Nutrition & composition

  • Provides carbohydrate, water and dietary fibre, especially when the skin is eaten.
  • Contains vitamin C and a range of polyphenol plant compounds; levels vary by variety and storage.
  • Much of the fibre is in the intact fruit; juice contains far less fibre than the whole apple.

Culinary characteristics

  • Raw texture ranges from crisp to tender; heating softens the flesh and concentrates sweetness.
  • Natural pectin helps cooked apple form a soft, cohesive texture.
  • Tart varieties hold savoury or baked applications well; sweeter varieties can reduce the need for added sweetness.

Uses & preparation

  • Fresh fruit, porridge, salads and snacks.
  • Compotes, baked fruit, sauces and fillings.
  • Grated into batters, oat dishes and savoury slaws.

Selection, storage & considerations

  • Choose firm fruit without major bruising; refrigerating generally slows softening.
  • Cut surfaces brown through oxidation; acid such as lemon juice slows this.
  • Wash before eating; leaving the edible skin on retains more fibre.
