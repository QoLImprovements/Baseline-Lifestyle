Aubergine

TypeFruit used as a vegetable (Solanum melongena)

Aubergine has a spongy raw structure that becomes soft, silky and savoury with cooking. Its relatively neutral flesh readily takes on sauces, spices and roasting flavours.

Nutrition & composition

  • High in water and provides dietary fibre with relatively little energy on its own.
  • Purple-skinned varieties contain anthocyanin pigments and other plant compounds concentrated in the skin.
  • Its nutritional profile changes considerably when large amounts of cooking fat are absorbed.

Culinary characteristics

  • Raw flesh is firm and porous; heat collapses the structure into a soft, creamy texture.
  • Readily absorbs oil, marinades and strongly flavoured sauces.
  • Roasting, grilling and long simmering develop deeper savoury flavours than brief cooking.

Uses & preparation

  • Roasted or grilled vegetable dishes.
  • Curries, stews, tomato-based sauces and tray bakes.
  • Dips, purées, stuffed dishes and layered bakes.

Selection, storage & considerations

  • Choose firm, glossy aubergines that feel heavy for their size.
  • Salting is optional for most modern varieties; it can still help alter surface moisture or seasoning.
  • Store cool and use before the skin wrinkles or the flesh becomes heavily browned.
