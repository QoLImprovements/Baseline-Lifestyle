Baseline Massage
Warm bodywork shaped around comfort, pressure, rhythm, flow, and receiving.

Use this page to settle the massage preference before the session starts. Areas, pressure, pace, timing, clear agreement, and changes can be named early, so the session is easier to shape and easier to receive.

How The System Works

  • Preference must be named: the receiver gives real information about areas, pressure, pace, likes, dislikes, and boundaries.
  • Pressure is feedback: light, medium, firm, changing, or avoided pressure are all valid, but the feedback needs to be communicated.
  • Time is deliberate: the receiver can choose broad full-body flow, one focused area, or a balanced session across several areas.
  • Agreement is active: sensitive or specific areas require clear choice, clear comfort, and ongoing communication.
  • Change is allowed: the session can be adjusted during the massage if something feels too much, not enough, or simply not right.

Communication comes first: The massage is not built for someone who refuses to communicate. The planner exists so the receiver can state preferences clearly.

Account-bound preference: When signed in, massage preferences are saved to the receiver’s account rather than browser storage.

Compression

Static compression

Overview: Still pressure held through a broad or focused area. It can feel grounding because the pressure is clear and held rather than slippery, making it useful when the receiver wants contact that feels steady and contained.

How it is performed: palms, thumbs, forearms, or soft fists hold steady pressure for a short period. The pressure can be still, pulsed, or slowly released, and it should feel broad enough to support the area rather than poke into it.

What it is used for: grounding, easing tight areas, and giving pressure without sliding. It can be especially useful when the receiver wants pressure but does not want constant sliding, oiliness, or a restless pace.

Variations: palm compression, forearm compression, thumb compression, and broad supported holds. A broad variation usually feels more supportive; a narrow variation feels more specific and should be checked more carefully.

Variations: palm pulsing, forearm pulsing, compression with rocking, and compression followed by glide. A broad variation usually feels more supportive; a narrow variation feels more specific and should be checked more carefully.

Effleurage

Long gliding strokes

Overview: Continuous gliding contact that warms tissue, settles the nervous system, and connects one area of the massage to another. It usually belongs at the beginning and end of a massage, and it can also be used between deeper work so the receiver does not feel jumped from one area to another.

How it is performed: palms, fingers, or forearms glide with oil or balm in long, even paths, usually following the shape of the body rather than jumping between isolated points. The contact should feel continuous and unbroken, with enough pressure to feel present but not so much that it becomes digging or dragging.

What it is used for: warming tissue, settling the receiver, connecting the session, checking how the body responds to pressure, and reducing abrupt transitions between areas. It is also useful as a transition, because it lets the massage feel close and connected rather than broken into separate tasks.

Variations: broad palm glide, forearm glide, light finishing glide, slower grounding strokes, and connecting strokes between nearby areas. The variation should match the area size and the desired mood: broad and slow for settling, lighter for finishing, firmer only when clearly wanted.

Friction

Circular friction

Overview: Small circular focused movement over a local area. It is more local than flowing massage, so it needs to be used with care, brief timing, and clear feedback rather than being repeated until the body braces.

How it is performed: fingers, thumbs, or knuckles make controlled circles without excessive force. Because the movement is small and specific, it should be brief, deliberate, and followed by softer contact so the area does not feel irritated.

What it is used for: local warming, small areas of tightness, and more detailed attention. It is best used as a focused tool inside a wider massage, not as the whole feeling of the session.

Variations: thumb circles, fingertip circles, supported knuckle circles, and lighter circles over sensitive areas. The smaller the contact, the more important it is to keep it short, specific, and balanced with broader contact around it.

What it is used for: focused texture work, tendon or muscle attachment work, and small stubborn areas. It is best used as a focused tool inside a wider massage, not as the whole feeling of the session.

Variations: thumb cross-fibre, finger cross-fibre, and short forearm-supported friction. The smaller the contact, the more important it is to keep it short, specific, and balanced with broader contact around it.

Lomi Lomi Techniques

Broad arm compression

Overview: Broad pressure delivered through the forearm, arm, or carefully placed elbow contact rather than narrow, poking pressure. It helps turn a general massage preference into something the provider can actually shape with pressure, pace, timing, and clear feedback.

How it is performed: the provider settles broad contact into a suitable fleshy area, holds or pulses the pressure, and releases gradually while keeping the receiver informed. The approach should be clear enough for the receiver to understand what is happening, while still being adjusted through comfort and feedback.

What it is used for: warmth, grounding, larger-area pressure, and a fuller contact feeling without relying only on the hands. This gives the provider a clearer reason for including it and helps the receiver decide whether it belongs in the session.

Context: this is a general library description of a method used in some lomilomi training; exact practice varies by teacher, family, and lineage. This point should be read as practical guidance, not a fixed script; the receiver’s comfort and communication shape how it is used.

Passive Movement / Gentle Mobilisation

Gentle range of movement

Overview: The provider moves an area while the receiver stays relaxed. It brings movement into the session without asking the receiver to perform, and it should always stay inside a comfortable, supported range.

How it is performed: joints are moved slowly through comfortable range only. The provider supports the limb or joint so the receiver can stay relaxed, with the movement stopping before strain, stretch-pain, or loss of trust.

What it is used for: checking ease, reducing stiffness, and helping the body feel moved without effort. It can help the body feel included and moved without turning the massage into exercise or performance.

Variations: shoulder range, hip range, ankle circles, wrist movement, and neck movement only with care. The variation should follow the receiver’s comfort, the area being moved, and whether the aim is relaxation, mobility, or a gentle reset.

Petrissage

Kneading

Overview: Rhythmic lifting and pressing of soft tissue. It gives the body a warmer, worked-through feeling without needing to become sharp or clinical, and it can be adjusted from gentle and nurturing to firmer and more focused.

How it is performed: tissue is gently gathered, lifted, compressed, and released in rhythm. The movement should feel like the tissue is being warmed and worked through, not pinched or grabbed, with rhythm doing as much of the work as force.

What it is used for: warming muscles, softening tight areas, and giving a nurturing pressure feeling. It can bring a satisfying worked-through feeling while still keeping warmth and care in the session.

Variations: thumb kneading, palm kneading, forearm-assisted kneading, and slower broad kneading. The same family can feel comforting, focused, or sports-led depending on depth, speed, and how much tissue is lifted or compressed.

Tapotement

Hacking

Overview: Light rhythmic chopping made with the outer / little-finger side of relaxed hands, not with a flat palm or hard edge. It is usually brighter and more stimulating than slow massage, so it fits best when the receiver wants energy, circulation, or a short change in rhythm.

How it is performed: the relaxed outer edges of the hands alternate quickly over a larger muscle area, with loose wrists and soft, springy contact rather than hard chopping. The side of the hand is the defining feature of hacking. The rhythm matters more than force: it should feel controlled, light enough to receive, and easy to stop if it feels too stimulating.

What it is used for: brief energising, stimulating larger areas, changing the rhythm of the session, or adding a brighter finish when the receiver wants that feeling. It can wake an area up, change the mood of the session, or give a more energising finish when that is wanted.

Variations: very light hacking, slower rhythmic hacking, short bursts only, or replacing it with softer tapping if the receiver finds side-hand percussion too sharp or startling. These variations should be offered as options, not imposed, because some receivers find them energising while others find them too sharp or startling.

Lomi Lomi Massage

Overview: A flowing Hawaiian massage tradition using broad, rhythmic contact to create continuity across the body rather than treating each area as an isolated task.

Cultural context: Lomilomi is a Native Hawaiian healing practice with varied family, teacher, and lineage traditions. A general massage-library description cannot represent every traditional form.

What it feels like: long flowing strokes, broad contact, compression, rhythm, and smooth transitions that can make the massage feel spacious, warm, and connected.

How it is performed: trained practitioners may use the hands, forearms, arms, or elbows for gliding strokes, compression, and long continuous movement, with technique adapted to the receiver and the practitioner’s training.

Why someone might choose it: full-body flow, rhythmic movement, warmth, relaxation, and a session that feels integrated rather than divided into separate body parts.

Pressure choice: contact may range from light and soothing to firmer and more substantial, but it should remain clearly communicated, comfortable, and adjustable.

Session direction: the work may travel in long paths across larger areas, return in repeated waves, and use broad transitions so the body is not left feeling fragmented.

Related library section: see Lomi Lomi Techniques for general hands-on methods such as broad arm compression, forearm and hand gliding, long flowing strokes, and continuous full-body transitions.

Naming and training: where a provider is not traditionally trained in lomilomi, it is more accurate to describe the work as Lomi Lomi-inspired flowing massage rather than presenting it as the complete traditional Hawaiian healing practice.

Clear boundaries: pain, injury, unexplained swelling, numbness, fever, medical concerns, and any culturally or personally sensitive element require appropriate limits, consent, and referral where needed.

Types Of Massage

Sports Massage

Overview: Focused bodywork with clear pressure. Sports Massage is grounded, practical, and clearly communicated, supporting recovery, movement comfort, maintenance, and areas that feel overworked, tired, heavy, or restricted.

Overall direction: pressure should stay useful, communicated, and adjustable rather than becoming forceful or something the receiver has to endure.

Scope: consultation, preparation, soft-tissue work, pressure awareness, stretching where appropriate, aftercare, and referral when something is outside massage. It does not diagnose or treat acute injury, unexplained symptoms, or pathology.

Focused pressure: firmer contact, compression, friction, soft tissue release, and stretching can be used, but the body should not be forced.

Useful for maintenance: training support, tired legs, shoulder load, recovery, movement comfort, and areas that repeatedly feel restricted.

Pressure can work in waves: broad pressure, focused contact, a lighter reset, then another round if wanted.

Clear boundaries: sharp pain, acute injury, unexplained swelling, numbness, fever, or medical concern needs appropriate clinical support.

Trigger point work: steady, specific pressure can be used carefully and briefly for local tightness or repeated holding patterns, followed by a broad reset rather than grinding pressure.

Soft tissue release: position, pressure, and gentle active or passive movement can be combined where an area responds better to movement than static pressure.

Cross-fibre friction: short focused friction can be used across the tissue direction with clear feedback, brief timing, and softer contact afterwards.

Assisted stretching: the provider can support the limb into a clear, tolerable stretch without forcing range, using static stretch, contract-relax, gentle range of movement, or shorter repeated stretches.

Gentle resisted engagement: a light contraction against support, followed by release, can help an area reset through active participation rather than force.

Compression for recovery: broad still or pulsed pressure through larger areas can support tired legs, shoulders, training load, and general recovery.

Massage Feelings / Themes

Lomi Lomi Flow

Overview: Broad, rhythmic, connected movement that helps the session feel continuous rather than divided into separate tasks. It helps turn a general massage preference into something the provider can actually shape with pressure, pace, timing, and clear feedback.

How it is shaped: long flowing strokes, hands, forearms or arms, broad compression, repeated rhythm, and smooth transitions, adapted to the provider’s training and the receiver’s comfort. The approach should be clear enough for the receiver to understand what is happening, while still being adjusted through comfort and feedback.

Useful when: the receiver wants warm full-body continuity, flowing rhythm, and fewer abrupt changes between areas. This gives the provider a clearer reason for including it and helps the receiver decide whether it belongs in the session.

Cultural note: this theme describes a Lomi Lomi-inspired session direction. Traditional lomilomi is a Native Hawaiian healing practice with varied family, teacher, and lineage traditions. This point should be read as practical guidance, not a fixed script; the receiver’s comfort and communication shape how it is used.
